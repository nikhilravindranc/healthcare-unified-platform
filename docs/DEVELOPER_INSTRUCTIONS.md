# Developer instructions: contacts, consent and merging

Read this first. It is the short version of `docs/IDENTITY_DECISIONS.md`, which has the reasoning, options and full data model.

## What the product is

EVOQ Healthcare is one platform for a clinic: Practice Management (patients, appointments, billing), Enquiries, CRM Contacts, Campaigns and Online Bookings, all on one set of records. The frontend is finished on a demo in-memory backend. Messages, campaigns and bookings are simulated. Roles and field masking exist only in the browser.

Your job is to build the real backend behind it, following the rules below. Product has already decided these. If something cannot be built as written, say so and propose an alternative, rather than changing the rule silently.

## Rules to implement

### 1. Identity
1. Every person has a **Contact** with a permanent, opaque, never-reused id generated by the server. The frontend never creates or derives ids.
2. A Contact is created at the first touch: enquiry, online booking or import.
3. A **Patient** is a clinical record linked to one contact. A contact has zero or one patient. `Patient.id` never changes.
4. An **Enquiry** always has a `contact_id`. `patient_id` is set on conversion. Several enquiries can belong to one contact.
5. A name, phone number or email is **never** an identifier.

### 2. Phone numbers and emails
1. Store them in a separate table: one contact, many numbers or emails. Normalize numbers (country code) before comparing.
2. Each has a status: `active`, `shared` or `no_longer_in_use`, plus dates.
3. One number can be linked to several contacts (family or friends). Mark it `shared`.
4. If a number changes hands, mark it `no_longer_in_use` on the old contact. The new owner inherits no history or consent.
5. Consent belongs to the contact, never to a number.

### 3. Duplicate suggestions and merging
1. Matching on phone, email, or name plus date of birth produces a **suggestion with a reason**. It never merges anything.
2. A merge is a separate action by a named person, with a reason. Who may do it:

| Case | Who |
|---|---|
| Neither record has a patient | Front desk, Practice Manager, Owner |
| One record has a patient | Front desk requests, Practice Manager or Owner approves |
| Both have patients | Practice Manager or Owner only |

3. Every merge is audited (who, when, why, which record survives).
4. The merged-away id keeps resolving to the surviving contact.
5. A merge can be undone for 30 days.

### 4. Consent
1. Append-only records: `contact_id, channel (email|sms|phone), purpose (marketing|reminders|recall), status (granted|withdrawn), recorded_at, recorded_by, source, evidence?`.
2. A withdrawal is a new row. Never edit or delete.
3. **No row means not recorded, which means not allowed to market.**
4. Launch scope: email marketing, SMS marketing, and reminders by email and SMS. SMS marketing is blocked until SMS consent is captured. It is never assumed from email consent.
5. Service messages about a person's own appointment need no marketing consent, but need a reachable address or number.

### 5. Sending and delivery
1. The server checks eligibility at send time: explicit granted consent for the channel and purpose, plus a way to reach the person. The frontend preview is a convenience and must not be trusted.
2. Delivery status (queued, sent, delivered, failed, bounced) comes from the provider through webhooks. The app only displays it.
3. The business has not yet chosen the email or SMS vendor.

### 6. Online booking (public)
1. Public endpoints are separate from staff endpoints. They return only service, provider, place, date and time.
2. Changing or cancelling works only through a **signed, expiring link** sent to the person.
3. Never reveal whether an email or phone belongs to a patient. Rate limit these endpoints.
4. A returning patient is matched only on exact email and exact last name together. Otherwise create a new contact and let staff decide on a merge.
5. A booking creates a normal appointment with `origin = online`, and a new enquiry with source "Online booking" for a new person.

### 7. Sources
1. One global list, edited by the Owner.
2. Store `first_touch_source`, `first_touch_at` and the originating record on the contact. Never overwrite them.
3. A referrer name (for example a doctor) goes in a separate `referrer` field.
4. Campaign attribution is a separate field from source.

### 8. Tasks and notes
1. One task entity: one assignee, one due date, one status, linked to a contact, enquiry or patient.
2. Notes carry author and time and are never edited in place.

### 9. Appointments and permissions
1. Appointments stay owned by Practice Management. Keep a proper status history (booked, cancelled, no-show, rescheduled, who and when).
2. The server enforces permissions and field-level access:
   - phone, email and date of birth need patient-data access or enquiry management;
   - patient ids and appointment detail need patient-record access;
   - Marketing sees name, stage, source and appointment date and status only;
   - message text needs enquiry management.

## What to send back

1. Confirm each rule above is buildable, or say what you would change and why.
2. Send the field names and response shapes you intend to use for contacts, enquiries, consent, messages, campaigns and public booking. This lets the frontend swap its demo data layers one at a time (`enquiriesApi`, `commsApi`, `campaignsApi`, `bookingApi`) without redesigning screens.
3. Answer the open points:
   - Which messaging providers, and who owns delivery status?
   - How will the signed booking link be delivered and how long does it last?
   - Which consent evidence do we store for a phone-recorded consent?

## Do not

- Merge records automatically for any reason.
- Treat missing consent as permission.
- Move consent or history from one phone number's owner to the next.
- Build on the CRM directory keys such as `patient:P-12`. They are display keys only.
- Trust the frontend's role checks, masking or eligibility preview.
